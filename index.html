Karthick Mohan Kumar

I'm a PhD student at the University of Maryland, College Park, advised by Dr. Shuna Ni. My research focuses on fire damage assessment, computational fire modeling, and machine learning for fire investigation.

Previously, I completed my MS in Mechanical Engineering at Colorado State University, where I worked with Dr. Shantanu Jathar on wildfire emissions sensing, optical measurements, and smoke-related data analysis. Research

I am interested in connecting fire science with computer vision, simulation, and intelligent sensing. My current doctoral research directions include:

Computer vision & materials
Fire damage assessment

Image-based characterization of fire-damaged materials, including gypsum wallboard, using machine learning and structured damage annotations.

Simulation & representation
Computational fire modeling

Exploring fire simulation and data-driven representations of fire environments, connecting numerical outputs with learning-based models.

Robotics & investigation
Intelligent fire investigation

Developing directions in robotic perception and interactive fire-scene evidence representations to support investigation research.

Selected projects

Engineering project
Wildfire emissions sensing

An IoT-integrated sensor pack for measuring gases and environmental conditions during aerial emissions monitoring.

Integrated CO₂, NOx, CO, pressure, humidity, and temperature sensing; developed enclosures, PCB designs, and firmware. Calibrated sensors against reference-grade instruments using barrel tests. Also investigated photodiode-based optical measurements for flame radiation and temperature estimation.

Engineering project
Learning-based HVAC control

A residential thermal model paired with reinforcement learning to improve temperature regulation and energy use.

Modeled the thermostat, heater, and building envelope using heat-transfer and thermodynamic equations. The simulated controller reduced average energy consumption by over 34% relative to traditional control models.

Engineering project
Processor heat sink optimization

Numerical analysis of transient and steady heat conduction across alternative processor cooling configurations.

Used Gauss–Seidel and fourth-order Runge–Kutta methods to compare thermal paste, finned heat sinks, and convective enhancements. The modeled configurations achieved up to a 35% reduction in core temperature.

Engineering project
Sustainable composite films

Biodegradable PVA films reinforced with Phoenix sp. natural fibers and ZnO for food-packaging applications.

Evaluated mechanical, thermal, chemical, and microstructural properties using FTIR, TGA, DSC, and SEM. Contact-angle measurements reached 79.5° with 5% fiber addition, indicating improved moisture resistance.

Engineering project
Lightweight UAV structures

Structural design and fabrication for the SAE AERO 2022 UAV competition team.

Led fuselage and wing design, supported aerodynamic validation through CFD, and conducted FMEA. Topology optimization enabled an 18% reduction in structural weight.

Engineering project
Hybrid vehicle powertrain

System-level modeling of a plug-in hybrid commuter sedan and its lithium-ion battery.

Co-developed a simulated powertrain targeting 45 mpg and a 40-mile all-electric range. Modeled drive cycles, battery behavior, and engine controls with an 80-cell battery across 10 modules.
